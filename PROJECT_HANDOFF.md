# Molar Quest — project handoff

Last updated: 2026-10-04

## Current state

The root route is a working single-scene pixel RPG. It deliberately uses the supplied reference composition as the playable backdrop because the earlier procedural canvas approximation did not visually match the requested artwork.

Implemented:

- native-ratio 1672×941 clinic scene;
- Claire directional walk animation with keyboard and touch controls;
- approximate environmental collision;
- gate-only fence crossing with free movement after reaching the sidewalk;
- clinic-door proximity prompt and interaction card;
- small white blue-eyed cat with idle/sit/groom/stretch animation;
- looping user-controlled background music;
- responsive HUD and on-screen D-pad;
- reduced-motion handling for cosmetic animation.

## Active game architecture

### Movement

`app/page.tsx` stores Claire's foot position as scene percentages:

- `x`: horizontal percentage of the scene;
- `y`: vertical percentage of the scene;
- `direction`: sprite-sheet row (`down`, `left`, `right`, `up`);
- `frame`: one of the three sprite-sheet columns;
- `moving`: toggles the bob animation.

Keyboard and pointer state live in refs. A `requestAnimationFrame` loop converts input to percentage-per-second movement and updates React state. Horizontal and vertical movement are tested separately so Claire can slide along obstacles instead of becoming locked on corners.

### Collision

`blockedAt()` contains broad percentage rectangles for the clinic, car, major trees, mailbox, and scene bounds. `canMove()` handles the fence as a crossing rule:

- fence line: approximately `y = 76`;
- walkable gate: approximately `x = 37..52`;
- once Claire crosses through the gate, horizontal sidewalk movement is allowed;
- lower movement currently extends to `y = 96`.

This is intentionally approximate. For a larger game, replace the hard-coded rectangles with a collision-mask image or authored polygon data.

### Sprites

Claire and the cat are transparent PNG sprite sheets rendered as CSS backgrounds. Their frame positions are controlled with `background-size` and `background-position`. The scene and sprites use `image-rendering: pixelated`.

### The four cats (house interior)

`app/house/houseData.ts` defines four cats, each with its own 4x3 sheet (362 px cells, same layout as the white cat: row 0 walks right, row 1 walks left, row 2 rest poses) and a behavior:

| Cat | Sheet | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Michael (seal bicolor ragdoll) | `public/sprite-sheets/michael-walk.png` | `elegant` — slow strolls in long lines, long rests |
| Aggu (white) | `public/sprite-sheets/white-cat-walk-concept.png` | `zoomies` — bursts of 3–5 fast dashes across the floor |
| Ollie (brown tabby) | `public/sprite-sheets/ollie-walk.png` | `hide` — ducks behind furniture, only ears show, peeks out |
| Sashi (blue point ragdoll) | `public/sprite-sheets/sashi-walk.png` | `hide` (upstairs) |

Hiding is done with the depth sort: the cat stands just inside the top edge of a `HIDE_TYPES` object so the furniture paints over it. The behavior code lives in `updateCats` in `app/house/HouseInterior.tsx`.

The michael/sashi/ollie sheets were generated by repainting the white cat sheet (Canva image generation with the sheet as reference), then cut onto the 362 px grid by `tools/characters/process_sheets.py`. The full-resolution generator outputs are in `docs/characters/generated/dl/`. Single reference frames per cat are in `docs/characters/generated/`.

The house view is 400x225 so Claire (40 px cell) is 10% of the view width, matching her size outdoors.

### Pocket Lite (2DS) edition

The game is called **Butterland** (renamed from Molar Quest on 2026-10-23; file names and the `molar-*` storage keys were left as they are). The handheld opens with `app/ds/opening.ts`: a short splash, a 4.8 s pan down from a morning sky (drifting clouds, sun, birds, butterflies, two hill layers) to the town, the BUTTERLAND logo dropping in letter by letter with a short chime, then a wooden PLAY / HOW TO PLAY menu on the touch screen. Any button or tap skips to the finished title; `prefers-reduced-motion` starts on it. Returning to the title with START shows the finished title, not the intro again.

Cats and ending (2026-10-23): Michael stays in the living room (`room` on his `CatDef`), Ollie lives in the basement, Aggu in the kitchen, Sashi upstairs. Hiding cats hide more often (80%) and for 8-15 s, and Claire must be within 1.6 tiles of a cat to find it (1.1 if it is hiding; `spotRadius` in `app/house/cats.ts`). On the handheld, finding the last cat brings the rumble of a truck coming up the street ("You hear a delivery truck coming up the street."), then a cutscene (`app/ds/delivery.ts`) shows a Butterfly Post box truck (modelled on a small Japanese delivery truck, 150x80 px, clearly larger than the Tesla) driving up, honking, dropping a package on the sidewalk by the gate and leaving; a "NEW PACKAGE! Claire got a new package! What is it?" notification slides down, the quest becomes PICK UP THE PACKAGE, and picking it up outside completes the game. The web edition has the cat changes but not the delivery ending. Footsteps are a soft 260-360 Hz thud in both editions. On first entering the house Claire says "Where are the cats?" before the quest is given (dialogs can chain with `openDialog(name,pages,then)`). Cats give a short, soft chirp-meow when Claire spots them and when she pets them (`makeMeow(...)(cat,happy,little)`). Debug: `replay(t,true)` holds the cutscene on one frame. Add `?debug` to the handheld URL for a `window.__cart` test hook (`state()`, `findAll()`, `replay(t)`).

Unboxing (2026-10-23): the truck and package are the owner's art (`public/delivery/`, cut out and scaled by `tools/characters/process_delivery.py` from `docs/characters/generated/delivery/*-source.webp`; they carry Yamato's black-cat logo and クール宅急便 lettering, fine for a private game but to be replaced before any public release). Picking up the package: Claire says "A package for me! Let's open it right here in the yard.", then `app/ds/unbox.ts` runs the unboxing beside her (box shakes and opens, an iPhone Duo rises with sparkles, a full-screen gadget reveal with a chime: pink burst with turning rays and sparkles, the unfolded iPhone Duo zooming in and floating, "IPHONE DUO!" below; then Claire spins and hops with music notes). It ends with `delivery="done"`, her line about the phone being paired with the Tesla, and the TESLA notice; from there A at the Tesla opens the phone's Tesla app (`public/phone/`, built in a parallel session). Debug: `__cart.pkgReady()` puts the package by the gate, `__cart.unbox(t,true)` holds the unboxing on one frame.

Narrative (2026-10-23): the handheld story is Claire at home with her four cats; the Pearl District and dental-clinic references were removed from the handheld and from the house's look-at lines. Outside is the FRONT YARD. The touch screen uses the title's meadow background (`drawMeadow` in `app/ds/opening.ts`) during play. The cat quest appears only after Claire first steps inside (a short intro dialog gives it); before that a WELCOME HOME card points her to the front door. The web edition (`app/page.tsx`, `app/layout.tsx`) uses the same story: FRONT YARD, a "Welcome home" start card, and the subtitle "Claire and her four cats".

`app/ds/cart.ts` is Molar Quest as a cartridge for the Pocket Lite handheld shell (`app/ds/pocket-lite-shell.html`, two 256x192 canvases with a `window.DS` input API). The top screen is 3DS-sized (400x240, filling most of the upper shell) and shows the world: Pearl District outside at 426x240 world scale with a following camera, the house inside at 400x240 with the same 40 px Claire cell as the web house and the bottom touch screen shows the quest log with the cat count and cat icons, conversations in a Mother-style box, and the action hint. Controls: D-pad walk, A talk/pet, B close, START title, SELECT sound; tapping the dialog box advances it. A sound ON/OFF button sits in the bottom-right corner of the touch screen; it mutes footsteps and meows and is remembered in localStorage (`molar-sound`). The shell's speaker grilles were removed.

Build it with `npm run pocket` (esbuild bundles `app/ds/cart.ts` plus `app/house/*` into `public/pocket/molar-cart.js` and assembles `public/pocket/index.html` from the shell with its demo cart removed). Preview at `/pocket/` on the dev server, or `node tools/serve-static.mjs public 8790`. Cat behaviors live in `app/house/cats.ts`, shared by the web house and the cartridge.

## House interior (added 2026-10-22)

Pressing SPACE/A at the clinic door swaps the outdoor scene for `app/house/HouseInterior.tsx`, a canvas-rendered three-floor interior in the Mother 3 log-cabin style. The outdoor loops idle while `scene==="inside"` and resume when Claire walks back out the front door.

- `app/house/houseData.ts` — all layout data. Each floor is a list of room rectangles (`rooms`), doorway cut-outs (`doors`), and placed furniture (`objects`). `CATALOG` gives every furniture type its footprint and whether it is `flat` (baked into the floor, walkable) or standing (solid, depth-sorted with the sprites). Furniture with a `say` array opens a dialog when Claire faces it. `CATS` places Mickey and Eggies on the ground floor and Sasha upstairs; each cat wanders its own floor.
- Painted floors (2026-10-22): upstairs now uses `public/house/floor-1-v2.png`, a copy with a stray orange strip across the spare-room bed painted out by `tools/characters/paint_out_upstairs.py`. The ground floor now uses `public/house/floor-0-v2.png`, a copy with the hall table in front of the stairs and the tall foyer mirror painted out by `tools/characters/paint_out_foyer.py` (original kept beside it). Each floor's `art` in `houseData.ts` points at `public/house/floor-N.png`, a cut of the owner's Mother-3-style repaint of the exported house map (labels and the static cats scrubbed out). When `art` is set, `buildFloor()` uses the picture as the bake and reads walkability from it in `readPainting()` (mirrored by `tools/characters/walkmap.py`, which renders a debug map): dark outline pixels (thickened by 1px) wall off furniture and rooms, a pixel flood fill runs from the floor's seeds (front door, stair landings), and a half-tile is walkable if at least 70% of its pixels were reached and read as floor; pockets under tables are filled in. Layout walls, doors and stairs are ignored on painted floors because the paintings sit up to two tiles off the layout grid. Instead `app/house/paintedWalk.ts` holds hand-authored rects per floor in the painting's coordinates: `open` (doorway thresholds the painter drew a wall line across, the front door, the kitchen mat), `block` (beds, desk, armchair, counter fronts that read as floor) and `stairs` (the painted staircases; stepping into one changes floor and the landing is just below it, and the stairs stay locked after arriving until the keys are released and Claire is off them). Walls and furniture are depth-sorted against sprites as vertical 8px strips (`runs`), each by its bottom edge. Cats hide only at painted furniture spots (`hides`) that have floor both behind and in front; they walk there on the floor and tuck in, so they never stand on furniture. Remove `art` from a floor to fall back to the procedural tiles.
- The basement layout was rebuilt to match its painting (56×26, six-row stone wall, partition at column 23).
- `app/house/houseDraw.ts` — `buildFloor()` turns room rectangles into a tile grid: the top boundary row plus `WALL_H` rows of stacked-log wall face, 1-tile side walls painted as vertical log posts, a dark bottom band, and tan plank / warm tile / concrete floors. Everything is painted with `fillRect` at 16px tiles — there are no interior art assets. `loadSheet()` shrinks the existing Claire and cat sprite sheets with smoothing and then snaps alpha so edges stay crisp on the canvas.
- Stairs are `stairsUp` / `stairsDown` objects with a `to` floor index. The flight is solid except its bottom step, which is the trigger; Claire lands just below the matching stairs on the destination floor. The front door is the `exitDoor` tile on the ground floor; `EXIT` in `houseData.ts` is the line she crosses to leave.
- The dialog box (`.mother-box` in `globals.css`) is HTML over the canvas: a name tag for cats, typewriter text in Pixelify Sans, a blinking cursor when a page is done. SPACE/A finishes the page, advances, then closes. Movement is frozen while it is open.
- Camera follows Claire at 480×270 canvas pixels and clamps to the floor bounds; the canvas scales to the world frame with `image-rendering: pixelated`.
- Dev only: `window.__house` exposes `state`, `teleport(floor,x,y)`, `tile(x,y)` and `snapshot(floor)` (a 1x PNG data URL of a whole floor) for checking rooms without walking there. `docs/map/house-map.png` is a labeled composite of all three floor snapshots.
- Outdoor changes made alongside the interior (2026-10-22): the roaming yard cat was removed (all cats now live indoors), and the active background is `public/pearl-district-playable-bg-v2.png`, a copy of the original with the DENTIST sign painted out. The original file is kept untouched beside it.

Art direction for the interior comes from the Mother 3 reference screens the owner supplied on 2026-10-22: stacked horizontal logs, vertical log posts, long tan planks with sparse seam ticks, dark-brown contours, cream/orange soft goods, shuttered windows, a potbelly stove. Keep new interior furniture inside that palette (`houseDraw.ts` constants) rather than reusing the outdoor coral/violet.

### Claire's phone (Tesla app)

`public/phone/index.html` is a self-contained pixel-art phone (folded iPhone Duo screen, 1 : 1.45) running a Tesla-style app over a pixel map of Pearl District: search, Recents / Places tabs, tap a place (list or map) to preview the route, distance and drive time. The car is parked at Claire's home at 1% battery, so Navigate always shows a NOT ENOUGH BATTERY dialog; nothing travels yet.

In the Pocket Lite game the phone is the delivery package: after all four cats are found, the truck drops a package by the gate, and opening it gives Claire the phone (`hasPhone()` in `app/ds/cart.ts` is `delivery==="done"`), followed by a TESLA low-battery notification. Claire is then told to walk to the car: standing by the Tesla (`nearCar()`) the hint reads USE THE TESLA and A opens the phone, unfolded (`../phone/?embed&duo`, a 536x408 phone shell laid over the bottom screen, edges cropped) while the game pauses; Y or B in the game, Z / A / Back / Escape inside it (it posts `{type:"phone-close"}`), puts it away. Y no longer opens the phone anywhere else. Picking up the package asks first ("What nice packaging... Open it here in the yard?" YES / NO via `askDialog`); NO leaves it by the gate to ask again. The debug hook (`?debug`) has `__cart.givePhone()`.
